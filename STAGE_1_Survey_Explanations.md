# Survey Summaries (2023 - 2026)

This document provides a brief, bullet-point explanation for each of the 12 extracted surveys and research papers related to the fertilizer recommendation and AI in agriculture project.

## Year: 2023

### 1. A Comprehensive Review on Machine Learning Algorithms for Crop Yield and Fertilizer Prediction
* **Core Approach:** Utilizes Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) and Gradient Boosting for evaluating agricultural patterns.
* **Data Sources:** Relies heavily on public agricultural databases, such as FAOSTAT and existing Indian Agri datasets.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** Models lack the ability to adapt to sudden, erratic climatic shifts (like unpredictable rainfall), highlighting a need for integrating real-time meteorological data.

### 2. Precision Agriculture: Deep Learning for Site-Specific Nutrient Management
* **Core Approach:** Employs Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) to make hyper-targeted nutrient recommendations.
* **Data Sources:** Fuses hyperspectral satellite imagery with physical soil samples.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** The system is heavily dependent on clear weather conditions for accurate satellite imaging, driving the future need for ground-based IoT sensor fusion.

### 3. Smart Farming: IoT-Based Soil Health Monitoring and Fertilizer Recommendation
* **Core Approach:** Uses established algorithms like Random Forest and Support Vector Machines (SVM).
* **Data Sources:** Gathers real-time Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), and Potassium (K) data directly from IoT sensors.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** High cloud processing latency limits real-time responsiveness, prompting the need for Edge AI integration for offline processing.

## Year: 2024

### 4. Advancements in Soil Quality Assessment: A Review of ML/AI Approaches
* **Core Approach:** Analyzes the effectiveness of traditional ML techniques including SVM, Decision Trees, and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN).
* **Data Sources:** Focuses on regional Indian agricultural datasets spanning the Kharif and Rabi crop seasons.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** Concludes that models acting as "black-boxes" hinder trust among farmers, suggesting the adoption of Explainable AI (XAI) for transparency.

### 5. Edge AI for Rural Agriculture: Low-Cost Fertilizer Recommendation Systems
* **Core Approach:** Introduces TinyML and lightweight decision trees capable of running on low-power devices.
* **Data Sources:** Processes data locally from on-site, edge-connected soil sensors to bypass internet bandwidth constraints.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** Faces a trade-off with reduced model accuracy compared to large-scale cloud models, pushing future research toward decentralized federated learning.

### 6. Blockchain and AI Integration for Transparent Fertilizer Supply Chain and Recommendation
* **Core Approach:** Combines Blockchain Smart Contracts with Support Vector Regression (SVR).
* **Data Sources:** Utilizes supply chain ledger records paired with regional soil quality indices.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** While highly transparent, the complex digital onboarding process creates a significant barrier for illiterate farmers, suggesting a need for voice-assisted UIs.

## Year: 2025

### 7. Fertilizer Recommendation for Agriculture using Machine Learning
* **Core Approach:** Leverages robust ensemble models, specifically Random Forest and XGBoost.
* **Data Sources:** Built around the Indian Soil Health Card (SHC) dataset encompassing N, P, K, and pH levels.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** Primarily focuses on broad macronutrients and struggles with localized micro-climate variations, necessitating real-time weather integration.

### 8. Generative AI based Fertilizer and Pesticide Recommendation System
* **Core Approach:** Uses Generative AI and Natural Language Processing (NLP) to provide interactive farming advice.
* **Data Sources:** Trained on multilingual regional crop and soil advisory datasets across India.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** Prone to AI hallucinations and relies on secondary advisory data rather than live sensors; future versions aim to couple directly with in-situ IoT sensors.

### 9. Explainable AI in Soil Property Prediction Models
* **Core Approach:** Merges Deep Learning with XAI frameworks (like SHAP and LIME) to clarify algorithmic reasoning.
* **Data Sources:** Uses IoT soil sensors that track NPK and moisture levels.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** The heavy computational requirement for running XAI limits its deployability in rural, low-bandwidth edge environments.

## Year: 2026

### 10. Magnetotactic Bacteria Optimized K-Nearest Neighbour-Tuned Tree Algorithm in Digital Twins
* **Core Approach:** Proposes an advanced Hybrid AI Framework paired with Digital Twin technology to simulate farm conditions.
* **Data Sources:** Fuses multi-modal IoT data streams with high-resolution satellite imagery.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** Plagued by extremely high setup costs and an absolute reliance on continuous cloud connectivity, making it difficult for smallholder farms to adopt.

### 11. Next-Generation Digital Twins for Hyper-Local Soil Management and Crop Advisory
* **Core Approach:** Pairs continuous Digital Twin simulations with Reinforcement Learning for dynamic, evolving crop advisories.
* **Data Sources:** Processes multi-temporal drone imagery alongside live, continuous IoT sensor streams.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** Demands extreme computational cost and robust 5G connectivity, driving the need for low-orbit satellite connectivity in remote areas.

### 12. Quantum Machine Learning for Complex Soil Microbiome Analysis and Nutrient Optimization
* **Core Approach:** Explores Quantum Support Vector Machines (QSVM) to manage highly complex agricultural variables.
* **Data Sources:** Analyzes intricate metagenomic soil data combined with traditional NPK metrics.
* **Key Finding/Limitation:** The sheer inaccessibility of quantum hardware prevents practical, day-to-day farming use, pushing toward future Hybrid Quantum-Classical cloud services.
